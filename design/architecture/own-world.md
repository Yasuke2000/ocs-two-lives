## 0. Why the imported map failed, in engineering terms

Every finding of the place audit (`scratchpad/place-audit`, 19/11, 58 anchors: 1 missing, 16 issue, 12 unrendered) has the
same root cause: **the terrain was made without knowing the builds, and the builds were fitted to terrain they could not
change.** The dwarf village floated 16–50 blocks because the jigsaw projected onto a surface the generator never made;
Dwargon flooded because Lerfing's water table sat above a hall carved into his rock; the Sealed Cave moved 113 blocks
because every candidate hit a keepout; Tempest stands on 45 % sand because the "forest" was a biome string painted over
open grass; no structures (hence no Tensura giant trees, no orc village) because the proto-chunk status skipped the
structure steps; everything "pasted on" because the only tools that could change terrain were the placer's survey, fill
and repair, after the fact. The fix is not a better placer. It is a world whose terrain is designed *from* the sites,
roads and lore, and then generated by Minecraft itself so that trees, caves, ores, grass, beaches and surfaces are real.

| Staging migration | `ttl-reset.txt` + new pack: the night pregen does the rest | Upload 5–9 GB per iteration (`import_world.sh`, 14 min) | Same as B |
| Iteration speed on a design change | Edit SVG → rasters (minutes) → `local.sh up` (~6 min) | Edit → regenerate hours → upload | Same |

| Main risk | Calibration of offset/factor to hit designed heights ±2; raster memory | Looks hand-made, not natural; two implementations of "what a biome looks like" | Status semantics (`noise`) are undocumented territory; aquifer/`water` surface conditions read a preliminary surface the generator did not make |

**Recommendation: A, with offline Python owning the design, checks and previews (so it is a hybrid in the tooling, not in
the generation).** Everything natural comes from the game, the geometry comes from us, every site is flat, dry and solid by
construction, and the whole thing is reproducible from a seed and a handful of PNGs that fit in `pack/config/ttl/`. B and
C both give up the two things the owner complained about most (real forests, nothing pasted on) or make us rewrite them.

### 2.2 The SVG layer specification (D2) — the lead's interface, with the converter's defaults

Each group is a `<g id="...">`. Coordinates are blocks. Attributes are `data-*` (Inkscape keeps them). Required attributes
are in bold; everything else has a default so ChatGPT can draw first and refine later. No transforms, no strokes-as-geometry,
no gradients; polygons closed; 20–120 vertices per path.

| Group id | Elements | Attributes (bold = required) | What `svg2rasters.py` derives |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `coast` | closed polygons, one per landmass | `data-name` | land mask (→ `canon_map.txt`: continent rule, nations, 128-block territorial waters); sea floor falls 63 → −20 over ~300 blocks with a seeded 8 % coastal wiggle |
| `lakes` | closed polygons | `data-level` (default: the terrain at the lake's lowest rim − 1, computed), `data-depth` (default 6) | basin in `height.png` (rim raised to level + 2 where needed), `water.png`, `river`/lake biome |
| `rivers` | polylines source → mouth | **`data-width`** (4–24), `data-head` (default: terrain at the source), `data-mouth` (default 63) | bed cut (level − 4), banks ≥ level + 2; at sea level a tidal reach, above it a chain of flat reaches stepping 1–2 blocks every 40–120 blocks; `water.png`, `river` biome; must end in a lake or the ocean |
| `mountains` | closed polygons; optional `ridge` polylines inside them (`class="ridge"`) | **`data-peak`** (crest y, ≤ 300), `data-rugged` (default 0.8), `data-shape` (`alpine` default, `mesa`, `dome`) | crest along the ridge (or the polygon's medial axis when there is none), flanks falling to the surrounding height with the shape's profile; `rugged.png`, jaggedness where rugged ≥ 0.8 |
| `hills` | closed polygons | **`data-height`** (y of the top), `data-rugged` (default 0.4), `data-shape` (`rolling` default, `mesa`) | plateau/rolling field blended over 64 blocks; `rugged.png` |
| `forests` | closed polygons | **`data-type`** ∈ `light`, `dense`, `dark`, `ancient`, `birch`, `taiga`, `old_taiga`, `jungle`, `sparse_jungle`, `cherry`, `mangrove` | biome where the `biomes` layer says nothing (§2.2.1) |
| `biomes` | closed polygons | **`data-biome`** from the allowed list (§2.2.1) | `biome.png` (wins over `forests`); beach/shore/river/peaks rules fill the rest |
| `regions` | closed polygons, one per nation | **`data-letter`** = a letter of `canon_regions.json` (J D W E P M B S H U R G F L O A N Y Q V I X) | `canon_map.txt` letters, nation markers; every land px must be in exactly one region (overlaps = error) |
| `roads` | polylines | **`data-kind`** ∈ `main` (paved, half-width 2), `trail` (1), optional `data-id` (default: `<from site>_<to site>` from the nearest sites at the ends), `data-bridges` (default: every wet run ≤ 64 blocks is a bridge, longer ones an error) | corridor profile in `height.png` (median, grade ≤ 1:2, cut/fill with 1:1 batter), `road.png` (class, bridge px) |
| `sites` | one `<circle>` per anchor id | **`data-id`** (an `anchors.toml` id, 58, each exactly once), **`data-site`** ∈ `clearing`, `plateau`, `carve`, `lakeside`, `coast`, `village`, **`r`** | `sites.json` for `CanonSites` (plate level = median designed height in r, or from the blueprint for `carve`; fade = r/2, min 24), `anchors.toml` x/z/y; `village` = a Tensura jigsaw landmark's clearing |
| `labels` | text | — | BlueMap labels only |

Pins and spots (11 + 9 anchors without a build) are sites of kind `clearing` with r 8: they only pin coordinates.
Claude-owned overrides per site live in `world/sites.toml` (`ground`, `fade`, `biome`, `facing`, `min_cover`, `notes`); ChatGPT
never edits it. The converter's `biome.png` rules, in order: `biomes` polygon → site plate biome (`plains` for
settlements, `meadow` for shrines; `sites.toml` can override) → Jura rings by radius from Tempest (core r 200
`tensura:ancient_forest`, band r 200..600 `minecraft:dark_forest`) → `forests` type → `river` on water px, `beach`/
`snowy_beach`/`stony_shore` on coast px by slope and temperature, `stony_peaks`/`jagged_peaks`/`snowy_slopes`/`frozen_peaks`
above the rugged thresholds, `plains` (or the region's default in `sites.toml [region.<letter>] default_biome`) elsewhere;
sea px: vanilla's own ocean choice by depth.

## 3. Generation

### 3.1 Terrain: designed height, vanilla detail

The pack already overrides `density_function/overworld/{continents,erosion,ridges}.json` with `ttl:canon_map` wrappers that
return vanilla (`outside`) beyond the ring. The new world adds three sibling overrides and leaves the rest of the vanilla
router untouched, so caves, aquifers, ores, surface rules and features are vanilla's:

| Override | New density function | Inside the design box | Beyond |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `overworld/offset.json` | `ttl:design_offset` | `(H(x,z) − 128) / 128` where H is the bicubic sample of `height.png` (vanilla's depth is `y_gradient(−64→1.5, 320→−1.5) + offset`, zero at y = 128·offset + 128; vanilla's own `offset` is `spline − 0.50375`, i.e. y 63.5 at the coast — same convention) | vanilla `offset`, blended over `blend` by `CanonGrid.canonWeight` as today |
| `overworld/factor.json` | `ttl:design_factor` | from `rugged.png`: flat 8, rolling 5, hills 3, alpine 1.5 (factor is how tightly the surface hugs the offset; vanilla ranges ~1–6) | vanilla |
| `overworld/jaggedness.json` | `ttl:design_jaggedness` | 0 except where `rugged` ≥ 0.8 (peaks): up to 0.6 | vanilla |
| `fluid_level_floodedness` (router entry) | `ttl:design_dry` wrapper | −1 (never flooded) inside every site box + margin and inside the Dwargon cavity volume: no aquifer water or lava there | vanilla |

`ttl:canon_map` on `continents` stays (the ring and the sea falloff); `erosion`/`temperature`/`vegetation` wrappers stay for
the free lands. Inside the box the climate channels shape nothing, because offset/factor/jaggedness no longer read them.

Calibration (WP-W2's acceptance test): a synthetic map with flat bands at H = 66, 90, 140, 220 and three ruggedness classes;
`/ttl site check` samples the heightmap on a 4-block lattice and must report mean |y − H| ≤ 1.5 on flat (rugged 0) ground
and the intended spread elsewhere. Because vanilla's 3-D noise still adds ±(2–6) blocks at factor 3–5, the look is natural
(no contour staircases); at factor 8 with a `flat` site on top (§3.3) it is a plate to the block.

Raster loader (`CanonRasters`, Java): PNG decode at server start from `pack/config/ttl/world/*.png` into `short[]`/`byte[]`
arrays, one bicubic/bilinear sampler, per-thread column memo (pattern of `CanonSiteTerrain.Column`). Memory ≈ 60 MB.
Load time < 2 s. `CanonGrid` keeps reading `canon_map.txt` (1 cell per chunk, generated from the same SVG) for the
continent rule, owners and the ring, so none of the ~10 script consumers of `ownerCell` changes.

### 3.2 Biomes, forests, coast, rivers, lakes

- **Biomes**: `CanonBiomes` gets a third branch before the palette logic: inside the box, the quart's biome is `biome.png`
  at (x/4, z/4). The rasteriser produces that raster from the `biomes` layer plus automatic rules: `river` over river px,
  `beach`/`stony_shore` on coast px by slope, `stony_peaks`/`jagged_peaks`/`snowy_slopes` above designed heights where the
  biome is `auto`, Jura's rings by radius from Tempest (the owner's Q4 rings, same numbers, now with trees), a site's
  `data-biome` inside its radius (e.g. a treeless `meadow`/`plains` clearing for a settlement; `tensura:ancient_forest`
  resumes beyond the fade). No patch noise, no palettes: what the map says is what grows. `canon_regions.json` keeps
  `regions` (names, colours, banners) and the J `zones` for `/ttlregion` and the arrival self-check; `biomes` palettes are
  deleted. Ocean biomes beyond the coast: `auto` → vanilla's choice by depth/temperature (the sea looks vanilla).
- **Forests**: real trees from the biome's features; **Tensura's giant trees are structures (`tensura:ancient_forest/tree`)
  and generate naturally** in `tensura:ancient_forest` because our chunks run every status (unlike the import). Density is
  chosen by biome: light = `forest`/`birch_forest`, dense = `dark_forest`/`old_growth_*`, Jura core = ancient forest, Treyni =
  `old_growth_spruce_taiga`/`dark_forest` mix (ChatGPT's region book picks; Claude maps to ids from the dump).
  `life/forests.js` is kept as a top-up tool (e.g. a denser ring round Tempest than the biome gives), not as the forest.
- **Coast**: the height raster falls from the coastline to −20 over ~300 blocks with a shelf; vanilla aquifers fill to 63,
  surface rules make sand/gravel by biome. Natural wiggle: `WIGGLE`-style 8 % of vanilla continentalness noise is added to
  the land mask edge in the rasteriser (seeded), so coasts are not vector-smooth.
- **Rivers**: at sea level (63) wherever the terrain allows — the lowlands are designed so the big rivers (Ameld, the
  western belt) are tidal reaches: a bed cut to 58–60 in the height raster, banks ≥ 65, the aquifer fills them. Above sea
  level a river is a **chain of flat reaches** stepping down by 1–2 blocks every 40–120 blocks (small falls), each reach a
  designed `water` level; see lakes for how it is filled. No sloped flowing water (unstable, ugly).
- **Lakes and elevated reaches**: the height raster carves the basin (floor = level − depth) with a rim ≥ level + 2; a
  placed feature `ttl:design_water` (step `lakes`, added to every biome through a biome modifier) fills water sources from
  the basin floor to `water.png`'s level in every column whose px is wet. Deterministic, per chunk, so pregen and DH see it.
  Because the rim is in the relief, nothing can spill; because sites never overlap water px (validator), no build floods.
- **Caves and ores**: vanilla noise caves and carvers on our terrain, Tensura ores by biome modifiers. Under a site the
  `flat` mechanism pulls the solid band (16 blocks) full; deeper caves stay (dungeons under cities are fine).

The converter then writes `sites.json` in the schema `CanonSites` already reads (`name, x, z, radius, fade, biomes,
terrain=flat, ground`) — the mechanism exists and was only emptied for the import — and `anchors.toml` gets `x, z, y`
per anchor. Builds switch to `ground = "fixed"` + `y` (already a supported `design/builds` key): **no survey, no
displacement, no fit, no skirt**; `repair.js` still runs but must report `foundation 0, skirt 0, lava 0` (the new acceptance
figure). Landmarks (Tensura jigsaw villages) get a `clearing` site too, so the jigsaw's surface projection lands on a plate;
the `fix-lerfing-placements` grounding code (median-of-real-surface grounding, placed-once, `unplace.js`) is merged first
and stays as the safety net.

Margins: site r = half the build box diagonal + 12; fade 24–48 (small builds) to 96 (cities). Two sites may not overlap
(validator) — Tempest's growth square (city, 192 × 192) is the site, not the village.

### 3.4 Roads

- The `roads` layer paths get a **corridor in the height raster**: `half + 3` blocks wide, the profile = the terrain's
  running median over 7 px, grade-limited (≤ 1:2, the existing `max_grade`), cut and fill applied to the raster itself, with
  a 1:1 batter on both sides. The terrain is therefore road-ready in every chunk the moment it generates.
- A placed feature `ttl:design_road` (step `top_layer_modification`) reads `road.png`: class px → surface mix (the
  `roads.toml` weights: dirt path/gravel/coarse dirt/cobble in Jura, cobble/stone/andesite/tuff on the Canaat stretch),
  a slab where the neighbour along the road is one higher, lantern posts every 40 blocks alternating sides, clearance of
  plants and leaves to y + 4; `bridge` px → deck 2 above the water level, railings, pillars (the deck block set from
  `roads.toml`). No cut/fill at runtime (the raster did it).
- `trade_roads.js`/`trade_roads.py` are kept behind a flag for roads added after the world exists (a nation's own road on
  the live server) — the planner reads our rasters at 1:4 instead of Lerfing's at 1:16.
- Roads in v1: Tempest→Dwargon (via the arrival), Tempest→Blumund (via the Spirit Gate turn-off), the Blumund→Englassia→
  Lubelius west road, Dwargon→the dwarf village spur. Others when their nations get quests.

### 3.5 Outer ocean and the vanilla world

Unchanged mechanism: beyond the design box, `CanonGrid.canonWeight` holds the canon ocean for `ring` blocks (set **1,200**,
the box edge is already ≥ 1,200 off any coast → ≥ 2,400 of open sea before vanilla land can start) and blends over
`blend` 800 into vanilla; `TerraBlender` regions as today. The design rasters fade their last 256 px into `CanonGrid`'s
deep-ocean continentalness so there is no seam; the sea floor is vanilla's within the ring. The free lands keep every
vanilla and Tensura structure; `onContinent` stays `ownerCell != 0` (land + lakes + 128 blocks of territorial water).

### 3.6 Structures on the continent

A structure-start filter in the mod (the pattern of the existing `PlacedFeatureMixin`): inside `onContinent`, only
an allowlist generates — `tensura:ancient_forest/tree`, small vanilla ruins (`ruined_portal` off, `trail_ruins` on),
`mineshaft` (keep: dungeons under cities), ocean ruins/shipwrecks in canon waters. Vanilla villages, pillager outposts,
Tensura wild goblin/dwarf/orc villages are off inside — ours are placed by `landmarks.js`/`builds.js` on their sites (owner
Q5 of 19/11, now enforced by code instead of by chunk status). Also used to keep a structure's bbox out of any site box.

### 3.8 The rasters as built (WP-W1, the interface W2–W4 read)

`tools/world/svg2rasters.py` writes `pack/config/ttl/world/` (`tools/world/build.py` writes `world/build/` and only
`--apply` writes there, together with `anchors.toml` x/z/y: that is the switch). `index.json` is the legend and repeats
every number below. Pixel (i, j) = blocks x0 + 4i .. + 3, z0 + 4j .. + 3, value taken at the pixel centre; rows north to
south. Box = content (land, lakes, roads, sites) + 1,200 of sea, rounded out to 512. The starter map: 3072 × 2560 px,
built in ~1 minute on one core.

| File | Format | Value |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `height.png` | 16-bit grey | `round((H + 64) × 16)`; H = designed surface = y of the first air block (offset = (H − 128)/128) |
| `rugged.png` | 8-bit grey | `round(rugged × 255)`; classes flat < 0.10 (factor 8), rolling < 0.45 (5), hills < 0.75 (3), alpine (1.5); factor piecewise-linear (0 → 8, 0.1 → 8, 0.3 → 5, 0.6 → 3, 0.8 → 1.5); jaggedness 0 below 0.8 → 0.6 at 1.0 |
| `water.png` | 16-bit grey | 0 dry, else level + 64; level = first air above the water (sea-level convention): fill sources from the bed to level − 1. Lakes, river reaches (one level per reach, steps of 1–2 every ≥ 40 blocks), plates under water. Not the open sea |
| `road.png` | 8-bit | 0 none, 1 corridor (graded, no surface), 2 trail, 3 main, 4 trail bridge, 5 main bridge; exact centrelines and profiles in `roads.json` |
| `biome.png` | 8-bit palette | index into `index.json` `files.biome.biomes`; 0 = `#ocean` (vanilla's ocean by depth and temperature) |
| `sites.png` | 8-bit | 1-based index into `index.json` `sites` inside every plated site's r |
| `exclude.png` | 8-bit bits | 1 site r + `data-margin` (default 48), 2 road corridor + `data-margin` (default 8), 4 carve cavity (box + margin + 8). Q4: no structure start where non-zero; `ttl:design_dry` uses bits 1 and 4 |
| `sites.json` | CanonSites schema | flat sites; carve sites left out (a flat pull would level the massif); lakeside/coast radius stops 4 short of the water |
| `canon_map.txt` | CanonGrid format | the chunk grid of the design box ('.' sea, '~' lake/river, region letters) |

A site's r is the SVG circle's, raised to the build's `min_r` from `world/sites.toml` when drawn smaller (§4.2: the
footprint drives the site). `map_check` uses the same effective radius, r_eff = max(drawn r, `min_r`; just the drawn r when
`world/sites.toml` is missing): it warns per site drawn below `min_r` and requires an edge gap = distance − (r_eff₁ + r_eff₂)
≥ the larger of the two sites' `data-margin` (default 48), because the generator keeps every site box + margin free of
structure starts. An anchor placed relative to another (`of`/`dx`/`dz` in `anchors.toml`) is one place with its parent and
siblings and is exempt from the spacing rule; it must be drawn within 32 blocks of parent + offset (warning). Carve sites raise the massif to y + roof + `min_cover` over every roof column (dilated one
pixel so bilinear sampling never dips under it) and cut the approach ravine through the roofless run of the box edge on
`facing`.
