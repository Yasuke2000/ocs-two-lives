## Selection and limits

Early official episode9 forest scenes replace hut/portrait leads for Jura; episode4 rocky approach and forge replace cave/campfire leads for Dwargon. Englassia has a classroom interior, Lubelius has meeting interiors. These supply visible material vocabulary, not street plans. Episode42 blue meeting-room walls/floor provide cold interior context; they do not resolve the White Ice Palace's coastline conflict. The Leon frame used for El Dorado is explicitly a persona palette, not that city's architecture. Episode40 portraits and episode45 skeletal battle images cannot stand in for polar or Imperial terrain.

Named secondary images of Londo, Siss, the cave, Dwargon gate, Elmine, Jia, Laura and Clayman's surface setting were inspected again. Their original episode attribution remains unverified. The image labelled Amrita is not an underground-city plan. The later Rimuru metropolis is basin/expansion context only. The official Coleus concept is local evidence for Coleus, not a template for Falmuth, Milim's domain or El Dorado; any reuse is limited to authored massing/material analogy.

Falmuth, O, A, N, Y, Q, Milim's domain, the Eastern Empire and El Dorado still lack a selected dedicated local landscape. Englassia, Lubelius, Damargania, Ulgracia and the Ice Continent have selected interiors or one component rather than comprehensive country landscapes. Four additional pinned fan-map files (Marris, Lune, Ice Palace and El Dorado) returned403; none was seen, mirrored, inferred from its filename or promoted into the register. The El Dorado file's alleged LN-illustration attribution is also unverified.

Five narrow official visual observations bring the ledger to247 unique records (189 thematic/design/reference +58 coordinate interpretations). They do not add directly read LN passages:14 printed-LN claims and3 separately classified author-side-story claims remain. No dimensions, country borders, biome percentages, named mountains or city coordinates are inferred from portraits/interiors. Region biome shares remain art-direction targets.
